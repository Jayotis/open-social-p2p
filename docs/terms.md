OSP is open-source software for a self-sustaining, peer-to-peer social network. You run or choose the computer (your "node") that holds your identity and content. The project aims to maintain and distribute software, not to become a permanent operator of network infrastructure, user accounts, media hosting or content distribution. Limited project-run infrastructure may help bootstrap alpha testing, but the intended network must be able to continue without it. Please read the important limits below before publishing.

## 5. Public posts, copies and deletion

OSP's current alpha is designed for public content. Public posts, profile information and media can be found by strangers and retained by other computers. Copies may exist on resharers' nodes, voluntary hosting nodes, in viewers' devices, in backups, screenshots or archives outside OSP's control.

Deleting a post sends a deletion through cooperating parts of the network. It cannot guarantee removal of copies already saved by someone else or by an uncooperative or offline node. Do not publish information that you need to keep secret, and obtain consent before exposing someone else's personal information.

Future private-sharing features, if introduced, require their own published privacy and security explanations; these terms do not promise private communication or anonymity.

## 6. Content prohibited in official OSP software

The following material is not permitted through official OSP applications, node software or project-operated services:

- Explicit sexual material, including pornography and explicit sexual acts or genitals shown in full.
- Prolonged graphic or sadistic realistic violence.
- Sexual material involving minors.
- Intimate images shared without the consent of the person depicted.
- Credible threats of violence against a person, or material created to incite that violence.

You must also comply with applicable law, respect intellectual property and avoid abusing project services, security mechanisms or other people's nodes. Ordinary disagreement and controversial lawful speech are not, by themselves, grounds for inclusion on the project's hide list.

These are rules for official software and project-run services, **not a claim that the project can police the entire independent network**.

## 7. Reporting, blocking and the optional hide list

You can report a post or person using [the reporting form](https://osp2p.com/report/). The project reviews reports and may act on violations of these rules. Reports alone do not establish a violation or automatically put content on a list. You can also ask for a listing decision to be reconsidered using the form.

Each node owner can block accounts, posts or files locally. During bootstrap testing, the project may delist material from a directory it operates or restrict access to a relay it provides. In a mature self-sustaining network, independent operators will make their own decisions about directories, relays, blocking and lists. No such decision remotely deletes material from someone else's node.

The project currently publishes a signed **optional** hide list focused on the most severe categories: sexual material involving minors, intimate images shared without consent, prolonged graphic or sadistic realistic violence, and credible threats. Adult explicit sexual material is prohibited in official software but is **not** placed on that hide list merely for that reason. Owners choose at setup whether to enable the list, may turn it off, and may make exceptions for particular people. An enabled list is checked by official software; a person's exception does not authorize redistribution of listed files.

Other people or communities may publish their own signed lists, independently of the project, as supported by the software. No permanent central moderation authority is intended. For details on the current policy and technology, see [ADR 0042](decisions/0042-content-rules-reports-and-the-project-hide-list.md). OSP cannot guarantee that every objectionable or unlawful post will be detected or removed.

The website and project-operated reporting or support services may process information you submit, and infrastructure providers may receive technical connection data. The separate [privacy page](https://osp2p.com/privacy/) explains the project's current practices and limitations; it does not govern every independently run node. Different privacy obligations can apply to different operators and jurisdictions.

You are responsible for protecting access to your node and obeying applicable data-protection laws when handling information about others.
